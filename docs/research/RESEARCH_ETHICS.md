# Research ethics

Start with synthetic or appropriately licensed public computational data. Record origin, acquisition date, permitted use, redistribution and attribution for each dataset. Store rights evidence with the task pack. A source being publicly accessible does not establish a redistribution grant. Escalate a task needing personal, confidential or restricted data for owner and appropriate institutional review before acquiring it or executing the task.

Keep task scope computational and bounded. Every task must be reviewed for scientific validity, input rights and execution scope. AI proposals are untrusted inputs. Research authors take responsibility for design, measurements, citations and interpretation. Preserve negative evidence and safety denials. Label synthetic examples, outside findings and measured AETHER outcomes explicitly.

Review logs and artifacts before any external release. Include software and benchmark licenses, permitted provenance, AI assistance disclosure, limitations, approval dates and all protocol deviations relevant to the conclusions. Meet venue and institutional policies before submission. Author and data contributor agreements are reviewed separately from the research verdict.

Distinguish numerical replication, independent verification and a broad causal claim. An evaluator can validate a registered rule for stated evidence. Claims beyond task scope require corresponding new evidence. Record conflicts of interest and compare alternatives under the same methods and budget.
