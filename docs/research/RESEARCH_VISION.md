# Research vision

AETHER asks whether computational research agents make better use of a fixed resource budget when they know which scientific claims depend on others. A campaign should yield a frozen protocol, a record of every attempted experiment, an auditable verdict for each claim and a report that a second researcher can check. The CPU classification fixture first establishes the mechanics of inspecting inputs and reproducing one calculation.

The study compares policies within one Rust harness. Their candidate proposer, available tools, task packs, resource meter and trusted evaluator stay matched. The principal research variable is how the scheduler allocates further investigation, independent replay, challenge tests and stopping. AETHER's engineering outputs and any empirical results are assessed independently.

Success has two levels. An engineering success packages valid evidence, handles incomplete trials consistently and allows an independent researcher to reproduce the measurement. The empirical hypothesis succeeds only if the preregistered comparison supports an improvement under the tested budgets and tasks. A null or negative estimate still supports a useful report when the evidence and limitations are complete.

The initial programme uses public or synthetic computational data and reviewed tasks. The first study considers classification, algorithm optimization and bounded reproduction. Each benchmark should have a computable or independently reviewable endpoint and known conditions for a correct negative or inconclusive verdict.
